# Research roadmap

The aim is to keep this a living, falsifiable and reproducible research project.

## 1. Independent replication

- Reimplement the definitions without using `analysis.py`.
- Reproduce the first 13 events and their recoveries.
- Compare every row and report discrepancies, including negative results.

## 2. Boundary extension

- Locate or produce an authoritative complete-search boundary beyond \(10^{20}\).
- Resolve the global record status of candidate event 69.
- Recompute recovery censoring whenever the boundary changes.
- Preserve versioned snapshots and exact provenance.

## 3. Recovery statistics and models

- Study recovery order, elapsed gap count, prime-index distance and number-line distance.
- Compare rupture frequency, jump size and recovery with explicitly defined prime-gap models.
- Account for right-censoring and the deterministic nature of the prime sequence.

## 4. Paper Two: prime-factor oasis tests

- Pre-register factor-structure measurements.
- Use matched ordinary gaps of similar size and numerical location.
- Test factor multiplicity, prime powers, sieve depth, factor covers, midpoint structure and primorial effects.
- Publish a null result if the apparent richness is explained by gap length and scale.

## 5. Mathematical communication

- Test whether the prime-step tree improves understanding of gaps, records, normalisation and the difference between observation and proof.
- Explore suitable OEIS submissions for derived sequences only after definitions and provenance are stable.

## Five ranked research questions

1. How do recovery order and complete-recovery delay change as exhaustive coverage grows?
2. Which specified prime-gap models reproduce the joint behaviour of rupture frequency, jump size and recovery delay?
3. After accounting for scale and skipped-set size, what variation remains in the last recovered level and completion distance?
4. Do matched ordinary gaps explain the apparent factor structure of rupture interiors?
5. Does the centred tree measurably improve learners' understanding of record gaps and finite evidence?
